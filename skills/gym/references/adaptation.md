# Injury Adaptation & Modifications

Load this file when the user reports pain, injury, mobility limits, age constraints, or missing equipment. Keep substitutions specific and preserve training intent.

## Common Restriction Profiles

### Lower Back Issues
**Higher-risk choices:**
- Conventional deadlifts with heavy load
- Good mornings
- Bent-over rows with poor form
- Sit-ups/crunches (flexion under load)

**Substitute:**
- Deadlift → Trap bar deadlift, hip thrust, cable pull-through
- Rows → Chest-supported row, seated cable row
- Core → Dead bugs, bird dogs, pallof press, planks

**Prehab:** McGill Big 3 (curl-up, side plank, bird dog) before every session

### Knee Issues
**Higher-risk choices:**
- Deep squats if painful in the hole
- Leg extensions with high load
- Box jumps and plyometrics
- Lunges with the knee traveling far past the toe when that position hurts

**Substitute:**
- Deep squat → Box squat to a pain-free depth, leg press with limited ROM
- Leg extension → Terminal knee extensions (TKE), step-ups
- Lunges → Reverse lunges, rear-foot-elevated split squat

**Prehab:** Banded terminal knee extensions, wall sits, single-leg balance

### Shoulder Issues (Rotator Cuff)
**Higher-risk choices:**
- Behind-neck press
- Upright rows (internal rotation under load)
- Deep dips
- Flies with excessive ROM at the bottom
- Bench press with elbows more than about 75° from the torso

**Substitute:**
- OHP → Landmine press, Z-press, Arnold press
- Bench → Floor press, neutral-grip DB press
- Dips → Close-grip bench, cable pushdown
- Flies → Cable crossover with controlled ROM

**Prehab:** Band pull-aparts, face pulls, external rotation work

### Wrist Issues
**Higher-risk choices:**
- Barbell front squats that demand painful wrist extension
- Straight-bar curls when they hurt
- Heavy pressing that requires painful wrist extension

**Substitute:**
- Front squat → Cross-arm grip, safety squat bar, goblet squat
- Barbell curl → EZ-bar curl, hammer curls
- Pressing → Neutral-grip DB press, push-ups on parallettes

## Age-Related Adaptations

### 40+ Considerations
- Longer warm-ups (10-15min vs 5min)
- More recovery time between sessions (72h vs 48h)
- Emphasize joint-friendly variations
- RPE-based progression over linear weight increases
- Include mobility work every session

### 50+ Considerations
- Prioritize movement quality over load
- Machine-based isolation can be safer than poorly loaded free weights
- Higher rep ranges (8-15 vs 5-8)
- Address balance and stability
- Regular deloads (every 3-4 weeks)

## Working Around Equipment Limits

### Home Gym with Dumbbells Only
- Compound lifts: Goblet squat, DB RDL, DB row, DB press
- Single-leg work compensates for limited load
- Tempo training increases difficulty without extra plates
- Supersets and circuits for conditioning

### Planet Fitness (No Barbell)
- Smith machine for squat/press patterns when a free barbell is unavailable
- DB and cable work for remaining patterns
- Machines for progressive-overload tracking
- Focus on controlled range and repeatable loading

### Hotel/Travel
- Bodyweight progressions (`references/workouts.md`)
- Resistance bands for rows and presses
- Furniture for dips, rows, step-ups
- Maintain strength; skip aggressive PR attempts

## Progression After Injury

### Phase 1: Acute (0-2 weeks)
- Rest the affected area
- Train non-affected areas when pain-free
- Prioritize nutrition and sleep

### Phase 2: Rehabilitation (2-6 weeks)
- Follow the clinician or physio protocol
- Use low load, high-rep therapeutic exercises
- Gradually restore range of motion

### Phase 3: Return to Training (6-12 weeks)
- Start at about 50% of pre-injury load
- Progress 5-10% per week at most
- Stop the movement immediately if pain returns
- Keep 2-3 reps in reserve

### Phase 4: Full Training
- Keep lasting modifications when they protect the joint
- Recheck that pain has not returned
- Maintain the prehab routine

## Safety route

If pain is sharp, worsening, neurological (numbness, radiating, unexplained weakness), or accompanied by chest pain, fainting, or unexplained swelling, pause gym programming and route to in-person care. Prefer clinician-guided rehab over remote load progression when those signals appear. This file substitutes movements; it does not diagnose injury.
