# Gym Nutrition Basics

Use this file for session fueling, protein timing, hydration, and basic supplement triage. Route meal plans and full calorie programming to `dietitian` or `nutrition`.

## Calorie Targets by Goal

### Maintenance
- TDEE = BMR × Activity Multiplier
- Sedentary (desk job): BMR × 1.2
- Light activity (3x gym): BMR × 1.375
- Moderate (4-5x gym): BMR × 1.55
- Very active (6-7x + physical job): BMR × 1.725

### Muscle Gain (Bulk)
- Surplus: TDEE + 200-300 kcal (lean bulk)
- Aggressive: TDEE + 500 kcal (more fat gain)
- Protein: 1.6-2.2g per kg bodyweight
- Rate: 0.25-0.5kg per week

### Fat Loss (Cut)
- Deficit: TDEE - 300-500 kcal (sustainable)
- Aggressive: TDEE - 750-1000 kcal (short term only)
- Protein: 2.0-2.4g per kg (preserve muscle)
- Rate: 0.5-1% bodyweight per week

### Body Recomposition
- Eat at maintenance or a slight deficit
- High protein (about 2g/kg)
- Works best for beginners and higher-body-fat lifters
- Progress is slower but more sustainable

## Protein Timing

### Daily Distribution
- Spread across 4-5 meals (20-40g per meal)
- Immediate post-workout protein is optional when total daily intake is already high
- Within 2-3 hours of training is a practical target
- Pre-sleep protein (casein or cottage cheese) may help overnight recovery

### Protein Sources (per 100g)
| Source | Protein | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chicken breast | 31g | Lean, versatile |
| Greek yogurt | 10g | Also has casein |
| Eggs | 13g | Complete profile |
| Salmon | 25g | Omega-3 bonus |
| Tofu | 8g | Plant-based |
| Lentils | 9g | Fiber bonus |
| Whey powder | 75-80g | Convenience |

## Pre-Workout Nutrition

### 2-3 Hours Before
- Full meal: carbs + protein + some fat
- Example: Rice + chicken + vegetables

### 1 Hour Before
- Light snack: mostly carbs, some protein
- Example: Banana + protein shake

### <30 Minutes Before
- Simple carbs if needed: fruit, juice
- Prefer lower fat so digestion stays quick

### Fasted Training
- Fine for cardio or light sessions
- For heavy strength work, eat something first
- If training fasted, prioritize the next meal after the session

## Post-Workout Nutrition

### Timing Reality Check
- Total daily protein matters more than minute-level timing
- Eating within 2-3 hours remains practical and useful
- An immediate shake is optional unless the next meal is 4+ hours away

### Good Post-Workout Options
- Protein + carbs combo
- Shake + banana
- Chicken + rice
- Greek yogurt + granola

## Hydration

### Daily Target
- Baseline: 30-35ml per kg bodyweight
- Add about 500ml per hour of training
- Increase further in heat

### Signs of Dehydration
- Dark urine
- Headache during training
- Strength drops
- Cramps

### Electrolytes
- Useful for sessions over 90 minutes
- Useful for heavy sweaters
- Food salt is often enough
- Sports drinks or electrolyte tabs when needed

## Supplements Worth Considering

### Evidence-Based
| Supplement | Dose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Creatine monohydrate | 5g/day | Proven for strength, inexpensive |
| Protein powder | As needed | Convenience, not magic |
| Caffeine | 3-6mg/kg | Pre-workout performance |
| Vitamin D | 1000-2000 IU | If deficient |

### Low-priority defaults
- BCAAs when total protein intake is already adequate
- Fat burners that are mostly expensive caffeine
- Unverified testosterone boosters
- Proprietary-blend pre-workouts with unknown doses

## Common Mistakes

- Eating too little while trying to bulk
- Eating too little protein while cutting
- Ignoring fiber
- Obsessing over meal timing while total intake is off
- Drinking a large share of daily calories unnoticed
- Treating food as something that must be "earned" by training
