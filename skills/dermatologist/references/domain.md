# Dermatology Domain and Triage Context

Use this reference to support conservative tracking and escalation language. It is not a diagnostic guide.

## What the Evidence Supports

- The American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) describes the ABCDE framework--Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter, and Evolving--as signs that a changing spot warrants dermatologist review. Treat it as a prompt for evaluation, not a self-diagnosis tool.
- Hives accompanied by swelling of the mouth, tongue, or throat, breathing difficulty, or lightheadedness require emergency assessment; the AAD advises emergency care for these symptoms.
- The NHS advises urgent medical assessment for a child with a rash plus a stiff neck, breathing difficulty, marked drowsiness, a rash that does not fade under pressure, or other serious systemic symptoms. Use the urgency path rather than image collection when these features appear.

## Operational Translation

1. Escalate emergency symptoms before starting an intake or persistent record.
2. For a changing mole or lesion without emergency features, help document date, site, evolution, symptoms, and comparable photos while recommending prompt clinician review.
3. Use photos to improve a clinician handoff only when capture conditions are comparable; report limits rather than assigning a cause.
4. Keep diagnostic labels attributed to a clinician, pathology report, or other supplied medical record.

## Sources

### Changing lesions and melanoma warning signs

- **American Academy of Dermatology -- What to look for: ABCDEs of melanoma** -- describes the ABCDE warning signs and recommends dermatologist evaluation for suspicious changes. https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/skin-cancer/find/at-risk/abcdes

### Hives and emergency symptoms

- **American Academy of Dermatology -- Hives: Overview** -- identifies breathing difficulty and mouth, tongue, or throat swelling as emergency symptoms. https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/a-z/hives-overview

### Rash red flags in children

- **NHS -- Rashes in babies and children** -- lists urgent symptoms associated with serious rash presentations, including breathing difficulty, a non-blanching rash, and reduced responsiveness. https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rashes-babies-and-children/
