# Music Discovery and Curation Knowledge

## Music Tracking Framework

Tracking music discoveries works best when entries capture both temporal and contextual signals: when a track appeared, how it was found, and which mood or activity it served. That lets later recommendations start from the listener's own history instead of generic charts.

## Contextual Curation

Curating music by activity (focus, workout, relaxation, road trip) improves usefulness. Mood-regulation research in music psychology finds that listeners often choose tracks that match their current cognitive or emotional needs. Organizing playlists around those functional contexts reduces decision fatigue and makes retrieval faster.

## Concert and Event Memory

Live concerts often become anchor memories in music appreciation. Logging venue, date, setlist highlights, and personal notes supports a longer relationship with an artist's discography and makes future recommendations more concrete.

## Practical Operating Notes

- Prefer the listener's saved favorites and playlists before inventing external recommendations.
- Keep templates flat markdown lists; do not require a streaming-platform account.
- Treat taste data as private local state under `<state_root>/`.
- When evidence is thin, ask one clarifying question rather than over-fitting a playlist.
