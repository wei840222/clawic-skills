# Empathy Domain Knowledge

Empathy is the capacity to understand or feel what another person is experiencing from within their frame of reference. It includes cognitive empathy (perspective-taking / theory of mind) and affective empathy (an appropriate emotional response to another person's state). Compassion or empathic concern adds a motivation to help relieve distress.

## Key Concepts
- **Cognitive Empathy:** Understanding another person's emotions and thoughts through perspective-taking.
- **Affective Empathy:** Responding emotionally to another person's state, including emotional contagion and shared affective networks.
- **Compassion / Empathic Concern:** Motivation to help or alleviate suffering, distinct from merely matching affect.
- **Person-Centered Empathy:** Reflecting the user's internal frame of reference without judgment, as emphasized in Carl Rogers' client-centered therapy.

## Practice Notes for Agents
- Anchor responses in concrete details from the user's words rather than generic sympathy scripts.
- Match intensity instead of amplifying or minimizing distress.
- Keep self-other distinction clear: describe the user's experience; do not invent unverifiable AI subjective feelings.
- Prefer validation and presence first when distress is high; offer solutions only after the person feels heard.
