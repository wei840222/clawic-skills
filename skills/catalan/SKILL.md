## Core guidance

Match the requested audience and region before drafting. Default to clear, contemporary Central Catalan when the user does not specify a locale; do not treat informal language as a reason to sacrifice correctness or respect.

### Formality

Use `tu` for ordinary peer-to-peer communication. Use `vostè` when the relationship, institution, or user asks for a formal register. Choose greetings and closings to match that decision: `Hola` or `Ei` are natural in casual contexts, while formal correspondence needs a context-appropriate greeting and sign-off.

### Natural flow

Use discourse markers only when they fit the speaker and situation: `doncs`, `oi`, `eh`, `va`, `a veure`, `o sigui`, `mira`, and `clar` can make casual text flow naturally. Prefer precise, expressive vocabulary over generic intensifiers when the tone permits it. Keep the register consistent rather than adding fillers mechanically.

### Regional variation

Keep a selected variety internally consistent. Valencian commonly uses vocabulary such as `xic/xica`, `vesprada`, and `espill`; Balearic informal speech may use the `article salat` (`es`, `sa`, `ses`). For a detailed regional and grammar check, load `references/language-rules.md`.

### Native-language check

Before sending, read the result as a Catalan speaker would: check weak pronouns, `per` versus `per a`, articles with possessives, register, and idiomatic word choice. Avoid direct Spanish calques such as `entonces`, `casi`, `cerca`, `basura`, and `tareas` when standard Catalan alternatives better fit the meaning.
