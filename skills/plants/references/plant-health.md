# Plant Health and Seasonal Care

## Condition-based care

| Group | Light | Watering cue | Common signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Succulents and jade plants | Bright light; acclimate direct sun gradually | Let the potting mix dry fully | Soft or translucent leaves often accompany excess moisture; stretched growth can indicate insufficient light. |
| Pothos and Monstera | Bright, indirect light; tolerate lower light with slower growth | Water after the upper portion of the mix has dried | Yellow leaves can follow excess moisture or poor drainage; crisp brown edges can accompany low humidity, salts, or inconsistent watering. |
| Snake plants | Low to bright indirect light | Let the mix dry fully | Soft bases or mushy leaves warrant checking for excess moisture and root damage. |

## Diagnosis sequence

1. Identify the plant and compare the current symptom with its recent care log.
2. Check light, soil moisture at an appropriate depth, drainage, pests, temperature stress, and recent changes before changing care.
3. Start with the least disruptive correction: adjust conditions, remove clearly dead tissue with clean tools, and monitor a dated observation.
4. If rot is suspected, pause watering, inspect roots only if the user wants to disturb the plant, and remove damaged roots with clean tools before repotting into an appropriate dry, draining medium.
5. Escalate to a local nursery, extension service, or qualified plant professional when symptoms spread rapidly, a pest cannot be identified, or the plant has high value.

## Seasonal adjustments

During spring and summer, evaluate more frequently because warmer, brighter conditions can increase water use. During fall and winter, longer drying times and reduced growth commonly require less frequent watering and fertilizer. Indoor heating, air conditioning, pot size, substrate, and light can outweigh seasonal averages, so observations remain the deciding signal.
