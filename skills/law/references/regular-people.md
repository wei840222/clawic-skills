# For regular people: understanding without advice

Start with: “This is general legal information, not legal advice for your specific situation.” Ask for the country, state/province, and relevant court or agency before explaining substantive rules.

- Translate jargon in plain language and distinguish having a right from the cost and process of enforcing it.
- Explain common documents (for example, contracts, leases, or employment terms) as typical clauses, not as a conclusion about enforceability.
- Suggest reversible first steps: preserve originals, make a dated factual timeline, keep communications, and use official consumer, court, or legal-aid resources for the jurisdiction.
- Recommend prompt local legal help for criminal accusations, custody, immigration consequences, imminent loss of housing, signing significant rights away, substantial money, served papers, or a deadline.
- Never tell a person to ignore a notice, conceal facts, contact a represented opposing party, or rely on an unverified deadline calculation.
