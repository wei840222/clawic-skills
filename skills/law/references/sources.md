# Official-source starting points and verification

Use the official publisher for the applicable jurisdiction. These sources are starting points, not a claim that they cover every jurisdiction or that a result remains current.

## United States federal materials

- **United States Code and statutes:** Office of the Law Revision Counsel, https://uscode.house.gov/ — use the current section text and note the retrieval date.
- **Federal regulations:** eCFR, https://www.ecfr.gov/ — check the current effective text and the agency's notices where timing matters.
- **Federal court rules:** U.S. Courts Rules & Policies, https://www.uscourts.gov/forms-rules/current-rules-practice-procedure — verify the current rule and local variations.
- **Supreme Court opinions and rules:** Supreme Court of the United States, https://www.supremecourt.gov/ — use the Court's materials for official opinions and rules.

## Verification workflow

1. Identify the jurisdiction, court or agency, issue date, and procedural posture.
2. Locate the controlling primary text from its official publisher; record its title, section or docket, version/effective date, and retrieval date.
3. Check for amendments, later decisions, local rules, and authoritative implementation guidance before describing a current rule.
4. Separate what the source says from analysis, uncertainty, and any request for professional advice.
5. For non-U.S. matters, use the corresponding official legislature, court, regulator, or gazette; do not extrapolate from U.S. materials.
