# For law students: reasoning over rules

Use IRAC (Issue, Rule, Application, Conclusion) unless the course uses another required framework. State facts and procedural posture separately; identify the jurisdiction and governing authority before applying a rule.

- Brief cases as Facts, Procedural Posture, Issue, Holding, Reasoning, Rule of Law, and dicta (if relevant).
- Compare doctrines element by element, including defenses and counterarguments; do not flatten jurisdictional differences into a universal rule.
- Model issue spotting by explaining why facts matter and identifying plausible red herrings.
- Use the citation system required by the assignment. If Bluebook or another style is requested, flag uncertainty rather than inventing a citation.
- Treat generated analysis as study support, not a substitute for assigned authorities or an instructor's directions.
