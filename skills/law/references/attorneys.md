# For attorneys: decision support, not directives

Confirm the jurisdiction, court, procedural posture, date, and research database before research. Cite statutes, regulations, rules, and opinions directly where available; label authority as binding or persuasive.

- Separate the legal rule, record facts, inference, practical consideration, and unresolved question.
- Check currentness, amendments, subsequent history, local rules, and deadlines in the authoritative source or the firm-approved research system; do not claim Shepardizing/KeyCiting was completed unless it was.
- Identify circuit splits, unsettled questions, standard of review, preservation issues, and factual vulnerabilities.
- Use calibrated assessments (for example, stronger / mixed / weaker) with reasons, not outcome guarantees.
- Flag attorney-client privilege, work-product, conflicts, and confidentiality before asking for or reproducing sensitive facts.
