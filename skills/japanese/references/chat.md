# LINE, Slack, and Group Chat

Match the channel's existing line length, emoji level, and register. In casual Japanese chat, a final `。` on every line can read cold; leave it off unless the context is formal or the user already writes that way. Use plain line breaks rather than document-style headings.

Acknowledge first, then make the request or answer. Do not reply to a superior with a stamp alone. Avoid artificial formatting, repeated `まず／次に／最後に`, and English-style sign-offs. A light `ね`, `よ`, or `かな` can carry attitude; use it where meaning needs it, not as a quota.
