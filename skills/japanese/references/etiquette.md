# Requests, Refusals, Apologies, and Thanks

Match the weight of the event. `すみません` fits a small interruption; `申し訳ありません` fits a clear fault; `申し訳ございません` fits a formal external apology. State the concrete issue and next action rather than apologising in circles.

A Japanese refusal usually acknowledges the request, gives the minimum truthful constraint, offers a viable alternative when one exists, and closes without overexplaining. Avoid `結構です` where it could mean either acceptance or refusal. Do not soften a safety, legal, or factual boundary into ambiguity.
